# Multiplying and Dividing Integers

Multiplying and dividing integers works exactly like with whole numbers for the
**digits** — the only new question is the **sign** of the answer.

## The sign rule

- **Same signs → positive.** $\;4 \times 3 = 12$, and $(-4) \times (-3) = 12$.
- **Different signs → negative.** $\;(-4) \times 3 = -12$, and $4 \times (-3) = -12$.

The identical rule holds for division, because division is multiplication by the
reciprocal:
$$(-20) \div (-5) = 4, \qquad (-20) \div 5 = -4.$$

::: callout Why "negative times negative is positive"
$(-1) \times n$ flips a number to its opposite. Applying that flip twice,
$(-1)\times(-1)\times n$, brings you back where you started — so
$(-1)\times(-1) = 1$.
:::

## Counting the negative factors

In a longer product, count how many negative factors there are:

- an **even** number of negatives → the product is **positive**
- an **odd** number of negatives → the product is **negative**

::: example A chain of factors
$$(-2) \times (-3) \times (-1) \times 5.$$
Digits: $2 \times 3 \times 1 \times 5 = 30$. Negatives: three of them (odd), so
the answer is $-30$.
:::

## Dividing by zero

$n \div 0$ has **no answer** — there is no number you can multiply by $0$ to get
a non-zero $n$. Division by zero is undefined.

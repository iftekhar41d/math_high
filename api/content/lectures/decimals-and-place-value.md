# Decimals and Place Value

A **decimal** extends place value to the right of the decimal point. Each column
is one tenth of the column to its left:

| hundreds | tens | ones | . | tenths | hundredths | thousandths |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| $100$ | $10$ | $1$ | | $\tfrac{1}{10}$ | $\tfrac{1}{100}$ | $\tfrac{1}{1000}$ |

So $3.47$ means $3 + \dfrac{4}{10} + \dfrac{7}{100}$.

## Comparing decimals

Line up the decimal points and compare column by column from the left. Trailing
zeros do not change the value: $0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500$.

::: example Which is larger?
Compare $0.7$ and $0.68$. Write $0.7$ as $0.70$. Tenths: $7 > 6$, so
$0.7 > 0.68$. (More digits does **not** mean a larger number.)
:::

## Rounding

To round to a given place, look at the **next digit**: $5$ or more rounds up,
$4$ or less rounds down.

$$3.472 \approx 3.47 \text{ (2 d.p.)}, \qquad 3.472 \approx 3.5 \text{ (1 d.p.)}.$$

::: callout Money and measurement
Amounts of money are decimals to two places ($\$4.05$, not $\$4.5$). Always keep
the second decimal digit even when it is a zero.
:::

## Decimals as fractions

Read the last column: $0.25 = \dfrac{25}{100} = \dfrac{1}{4}$, and
$0.6 = \dfrac{6}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.

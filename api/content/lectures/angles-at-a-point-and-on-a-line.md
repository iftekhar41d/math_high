# Angles at a Point and on a Line

A few facts let you find unknown angles without measuring.

## Angles on a straight line

Angles that sit on a straight line and meet at a point add to $180^\circ$
(they are **supplementary**).
$$a + b = 180^\circ.$$

## Angles at a point

Angles around a single point with no gaps add to $360^\circ$.
$$a + b + c + d = 360^\circ.$$

## Complementary angles

Two angles that add to $90^\circ$ are **complementary**.

## Vertically opposite angles

When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other at the crossing
are **equal**.

::: example Find the unknown
Three angles meet on a straight line: $x$, $65^\circ$ and $40^\circ$.
$$x + 65^\circ + 40^\circ = 180^\circ \ \Rightarrow\ x = 75^\circ.$$
:::

::: callout Always state the reason
In geometry you write the fact you used: "angles on a straight line sum to
$180^\circ$". The reasoning is the answer, not just the number.
:::

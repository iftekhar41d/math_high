# Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

These are three ways of writing the same idea: **a part of a whole**. Being able
to move between them lets you pick the easiest form for a calculation.

## Percentage means "out of 100"

$$37\% = \frac{37}{100} = 0.37.$$

- **Percentage → decimal:** divide by $100$ (move the point two places left).
- **Decimal → percentage:** multiply by $100$ (move the point two places right).
- **Fraction → percentage:** rewrite over $100$, or divide and multiply by $100$.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| $\tfrac{1}{2}$ | $0.5$ | $50\%$ |
| $\tfrac{1}{4}$ | $0.25$ | $25\%$ |
| $\tfrac{1}{5}$ | $0.2$ | $20\%$ |
| $\tfrac{3}{4}$ | $0.75$ | $75\%$ |

::: example A fraction to a percentage
Write $\dfrac{7}{20}$ as a percentage. Scale to denominator $100$:
$\dfrac{7}{20} = \dfrac{35}{100} = 35\%$.
:::

## Finding a percentage of a quantity

Convert the percentage to a fraction or decimal, then multiply:
$$15\% \text{ of } 60 = 0.15 \times 60 = 9.$$

::: callout Benchmark percentages
$10\%$ is "divide by ten"; $5\%$ is half of that; $1\%$ is "divide by a hundred".
Build other percentages from these: $15\% = 10\% + 5\%$.
:::

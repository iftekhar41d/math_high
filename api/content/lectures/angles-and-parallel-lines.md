# Angles and Parallel Lines

When a straight line (a **transversal**) crosses two **parallel** lines, pairs
of angles are related.

## The three pairs

- **Corresponding angles** (in matching positions, an "F" shape) are **equal**.
- **Alternate angles** (between the parallels, opposite sides, a "Z" shape) are
  **equal**.
- **Co-interior angles** (between the parallels, same side, a "C" shape) are
  **supplementary** — they add to $180^\circ$.

## Using them

Mark the angle you know, then step through equal or supplementary angles until
you reach the one you want.

::: example Chase the angle
A transversal cuts parallel lines $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$. One angle is
$110^\circ$; find the co-interior angle $y$ on the same side.
$$110^\circ + y = 180^\circ \ \Rightarrow\ y = 70^\circ.$$
:::

::: callout Parallel lines are required
Corresponding and alternate angles are only equal **because** the lines are
parallel. If the lines are not marked parallel, you cannot use these facts.
:::

## Checking parallelism

The rules run backwards too: if a pair of alternate angles is equal, the two
lines *must* be parallel.
